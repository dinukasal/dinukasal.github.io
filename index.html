About

I started coding in 2008 with Visual Basic and learnt the software engineering principles and basics at the University of Moratuwa, Sri Lanka, between 2012 and 2018. After uni, four friends and I worked on three startups and failed all of them. Later I joined Sysco, where I made life more stable, and I'm now with the ScreenApp.io team.

I specialise in software product engineering. I was a technical lead in the data reporting team at Sysco, and a member of the product that won runners-up at Microsoft Imagine Cup 2016 — the same product was picked as one of the world's top 20 engineering products at the International Cultural and Academic Meeting of Engineering Students (ICAMES) at Boğaziçi University, Istanbul.

I was also in the first Sri Lankan team to attend Hack the North at the University of Waterloo, the biggest hackathon in Canada, where we placed in the top 50. I'm really passionate about software engineering and building IoT projects.

Research

Occasionally I run an experiment properly, write down the numbers and publish the result — even when the result is "this upgrade made it worse".

  • Kepler GK106 (GTX 660) on Ubuntu 24.04 → A 2012 GPU in 2026: driver 470 against modern kernels, the NVENC API-version gate, GPU passthrough VBIOS failures, and why tensor-core unlocks cannot apply.
  • YOLOv8 vs YOLO11 for CCTV Person Detection → Benchmarked on 30 real security-camera frames: YOLO11 is faster and more confident, yet detected fewer people than YOLOv8n — including people plainly in view.
